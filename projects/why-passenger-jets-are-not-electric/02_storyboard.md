# Scene-by-Scene Storyboard and Visual Direction

Each shot lists: **Time · VO** (script cue) · **Visual** · **Camera** · **Motion graphics** · **On-screen text** · **Sound** · **Transition**.
Colour code (never break it): **AMBER** = fuel/combustion · **CYAN** = battery/electric · **RED** = limit exceeded.
Shot lengths are targets; final timing follows the recorded VO.

**62 storyboard beats.** Many beats contain 2–4 cuts or a major move, so the picture changes about every 3–5 s on average (fastest in the hook; longer holds only on reveals).

## SECTION 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:30)
*Goal: awe, then a question the viewer can't leave without answering.*
*Letterboxed 2.39:1 for the first 29 s; the bars open to 16:9 on the title hit (a subtle "the film begins" signal).*

### S1.1 · 0:00–0:04 · *(no VO)*
- **Visual:** Golden-hour runway, 600 mm telephoto, looking straight down the centreline. Heat haze boils off the asphalt. A narrow-body airliner (generic livery) sits at the threshold, distorted by the haze, landing lights blazing.
- **Camera:** Locked-off long lens, imperceptible push (1%).
- **MG:** None.
- **Sound:** Distant airport ambience (wind, far-off APU whine, a faint ATC radio squelch). At 0:02, the engines begin spooling: rising turbine whine plus a sub-bass swell.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on the throttle-up roar.

### S1.2 · 0:04–0:11 · "Right now, thousands of passenger jets…"
- **Visual:** Takeoff roll. Ground-level camera beside the runway edge lights; the jet thunders past at frame left, filling the frame. The exhaust plume distorts the background; grass and dust whip. Cut to rotation from a low side angle: nose lifts, main gear oleos extend, wheels still spinning as they leave the ground.
- **Camera:** Fast whip-pan tracking the aircraft, then a locked low angle for rotation.
- **MG:** None. Let the image breathe.
- **Sound:** Full takeoff roar, sub-bass rumble you *feel* (LFE). Doppler shift as it passes. Ducks under VO but stays physical.
- **Transition:** Cut.

### S1.3 · 0:11–0:17 · "We've electrified cars. Buses. Scooters. Even trucks."
- **Visual:** Four quick, beautiful, realistic vignettes (≈1.3 s each), each ending on a cyan charge-port glow: an EV plugging in at night (charge LED pulses cyan); an electric city bus in rain; a scooter's display lighting up; an electric semi truck at a charging depot.
- **Camera:** Macro push-ins on the cyan light in each.
- **MG:** A thin cyan "charging" hairline sweeps under each vignette, a subtle visual rhyme.
- **Sound:** Each vignette gets a soft electrical *tick* + hum chord stab, rising in pitch (musical cue, 4 notes). Silence after "trucks" (held beat).
- **Transition:** Match cut: the last cyan charge-port ring becomes a jet engine's circular intake.

### S1.4 · 0:17–0:23 · "So why is it… burning kerosene?"
- **Visual:** Inside the cabin during takeoff: a passenger's hand gripping an armrest, the window showing the wing and engine with the runway blurring. Cut to the engine exhaust core: shimmering, glowing faintly amber.
- **Camera:** Handheld cabin shot with real vibration; then a slow macro push into the exhaust shimmer.
- **MG:** None.
- **Sound:** Muffled cabin rumble, seat rattle; on "kerosene", a low amber "burn" whoosh.
- **Transition:** Cut.

### S1.5 · 0:23–0:29 · "…one brutal number…"
- **Visual:** The airliner in profile against the sky. Its skin flickers into **X-ray mode** for a split second, revealing wing fuel tanks glowing **amber**. Then, ghosted, the tanks turn **cyan** and **battery modules flood** the fuselage. The aircraft visibly **sags** (gear compresses, wings droop). Snap back to normal.
- **Camera:** Slow orbit, 10°.
- **MG:** Barely-there HUD corner brackets snap onto the aircraft; a single hidden number blurs past (out of focus), a teaser of "≈20×".
- **Sound:** HUD click, electric hum swell, a metallic *groan* as it sags, then a riser.
- **Transition:** Riser into a hard cut to black.

### S1.6 · 0:29–0:32 · *(title)*
- **Visual:** Black. Title assembles in light: **WHY PASSENGER JETS ARE NOT ELECTRIC**. "JETS" is lit amber, "ELECTRIC" cyan. Behind the type, a faint engine-fan silhouette rotates slowly. Letterbox bars open to 16:9.
- **Sound:** Deep sub boom + reverse cymbal tail; music cue 1 drops in.
- **Transition:** Whoosh into Section 2.

## SECTION 2 — YES, ELECTRIC PLANES EXIST (0:30–1:15)
*Goal: respect the viewer ("you might think it's impossible, it isn't") and narrow the question to BIG jets.*

### S2.1 · 0:32–0:35 · "First, electric planes are real."
- **Visual:** A quiet grass airfield at morning. A small white two-seat electric trainer taxis silently into frame. **No engine noise**, which is itself striking after the jet roar.
- **Camera:** Low-angle slider, shallow DOF, dew on the grass.
- **MG:** Chapter marker (lower-left): `01 — ELECTRIC FLIGHT IS REAL`.
- **Sound:** Birds, a soft electric whir and propeller *whup-whup*. Music thins to a sparse pulse.

### S2.2 · 0:35–0:45 · Velis Electro
- **Visual:** The trainer in flight over farmland. Then an **X-ray reveal**: the skin fades to show the battery packs (in the nose and behind the cockpit) glowing cyan, cables running to the motor in the nose.
- **Camera:** Air-to-air formation shot; slow 15° orbit during X-ray.
- **MG:** Spec card tracked to the aircraft: `PIPISTREL VELIS ELECTRO` · `First type-certified electric aircraft (2020)` · `2 SEATS` · `≈50 MIN FLIGHT`. A circular endurance timer animates a 50-minute sweep.
- **Sound:** Prop whir, HUD clicks as each spec line appears.

### S2.3 · 0:45–0:55 · seaplane + Rolls-Royce racer
- **Visual:** (a) A float-equipped bush plane lifting off a fjord-like inlet, spray trailing; (b) a sleek dark-blue single-seat racer slicing past at extreme speed, low over the sea.
- **Camera:** (a) water-level camera, spray hits the lens; (b) tracking shot with heavy motion blur, then a speed-ramp freeze.
- **MG:** (b) Speed readout spins up: `555.9 km/h` with label `ALL-ELECTRIC SPEED RECORD · 2021`.
- **Sound:** Water spray, electric whine; racer: sharp high-frequency electric scream + whoosh-by, then a freeze "thump".

### S2.4 · 0:55–1:04 · Solar plane
- **Visual:** Dusk. A vast, delicate solar aircraft (huge wingspan, tiny pod) floats over the ocean. A ghosted **747 wireframe** overlays it, and the solar plane's wings extend *past* the 747's wingtips. Then push into the tiny cockpit pod: one pilot.
- **Camera:** Wide drone shot, then slow push into the pod.
- **MG:** Span comparison callouts: `SOLAR PLANE · 72 m WINGSPAN` vs `BOEING 747-400 · ≈64 m`. Then a small label: `PASSENGERS: 0 · CREW: 1`.
- **Sound:** Wind, near-silent motors. On "one pilot", a soft single piano note.

### S2.5 · 1:04–1:08 · "…it clearly can."
- **Visual:** Montage callback: the four electric aircraft drift into a dark **Map Table** as small glowing models; their range rings appear: all tiny circles around their bases.
- **Camera:** Overhead crane descending.
- **MG:** Small cyan range rings (≈50–200 km).
- **Sound:** Soft UI blips per ring.

### S2.6 · 1:08–1:15 · "Can it fly THIS?"
- **Visual:** The camera rises from the map table and **a full-size A320-class airliner** rolls into a hangar behind it. The small aircraft models are dwarfed. Hangar lights bang on one bank at a time.
- **Camera:** Crane up and back to reveal scale; 24 mm.
- **MG:** Three stats slam in, one per spoken phrase, tracked to the fuselage: `79 TONNES` · `180 PEOPLE` · `800 km/h`.
- **Sound:** Each hangar light bank = deep *CHUNK* (relay + reverb). Each stat = bass hit, escalating. Riser.
- **Transition:** Whoosh + light flare into Section 3.

## SECTION 3 — WHY PASSENGER JETS NEED SO MUCH ENERGY (1:15–2:15)
*Goal: make the viewer FEEL the scale of energy, through mechanics.*

### S3.1 · 1:15–1:20 · "…what an airliner is actually doing."
- **Visual:** The hero A320 at the runway threshold at dusk, side-on, in **X-ray mode**: skin transparent, wing tanks glowing amber, fuel lines running to both engines like veins.
- **Camera:** Slow lateral dolly along the fuselage.
- **MG:** Chapter marker: `02 — THE ENERGY PROBLEM`. Labels tracked in 3D: `WING TANKS`, `CENTRE TANK`, `FUEL LINES → ENGINES`.
- **Sound:** Engine idle hum; fuel-flow trickle sound design (subtle liquid in pipes).

### S3.2 · 1:20–1:31 · Takeoff
- **Visual:** Takeoff roll, seen from above-and-behind (chase drone). Aircraft accelerates.
- **Camera:** Chase drone, matched speed, slight lag.
- **MG:** Bottom HUD strip with a **speed tape** (0 → 270 km/h) and a **timer** (0 → 30 s); weight readout fixed at `79 t`. An amber "power" bar at the engines pulses at maximum.
- **On-screen:** `0 → 270 km/h IN ≈30 s`.
- **Sound:** Takeoff roar, rising whine synced to the speed tape, HUD ticks every 5 s.

### S3.3 · 1:31–1:39 · Engine cutaway (★ hero engine shot)
- **Visual:** Cut to a static engine on a stand in a dark set. The **nacelle splits open** (exploded view on physical easing), revealing the fan, then a **cross-section slice** travels front-to-back: fan → compressor stages (blades spinning, increasingly small and dense) → **combustor** (ring of amber flame) → turbines → nozzle. Airflow rendered as fine particle streaks: cold blue bypass air around the outside, hot amber core flow through the middle.
- **Camera:** Macro slide along the axis of the engine, ending on the flame ring.
- **MG:** Minimal labels: `FAN ≈2 m`, `COMPRESSOR`, `COMBUSTOR >1,000 °C`, `TURBINE`. A thin arrow shows: `TURBINE SPINS THE FAN`.
- **Sound:** Deep turbine drone, the *roar* of combustion (filtered), metallic blade shimmer.

### S3.4 · 1:39–1:46 · "…tens of megawatts… a small town."
- **Visual:** Pull back from the engine; its glowing amber energy stream stretches out across a night landscape and **lights up a small town** street by street.
- **Camera:** Smooth pull-out to aerial.
- **MG:** `TAKEOFF POWER: TENS OF MEGAWATTS` · `≈ A SMALL TOWN`.
- **Sound:** Electrical surge, the town "powering on" (buzzing streetlights in sequence).

### S3.5 · 1:46–1:52 · The climb
- **Visual:** The aircraft climbs steeply through a cloud layer into sunlight.
- **Camera:** Side-on long lens tracking up.
- **MG:** Vertical **altitude ruler** on the right side: `0 → 11 km`. A ghosted Mount Everest silhouette (8.8 km) passes below the marker for scale.
- **On-screen:** `LIFTING ≈75 t TO 11 km`.
- **Sound:** Climbing engine note, air rush, cloud "whoomph" as it breaks through.

### S3.6 · 1:52–2:00 · Cruise and drag
- **Visual:** Cruise at altitude. Contrails form. A subtle **airflow visualisation** (smoke-streak lines) wraps around the nose and wings, with turbulent eddies behind.
- **Camera:** Slow air-to-air orbit; earth curvature visible.
- **MG:** A small `DRAG` arrow pushing backward vs a `THRUST` arrow forward, perfectly balanced. A distance counter ticks up quickly.
- **Sound:** Steady high-altitude cruise drone, wind hiss.

### S3.7 · 2:00–2:10 · 200 MWh = 3,000 EV batteries
- **Visual:** X-ray again: tanks filling with amber liquid (slosh sim). Then the fuel volume **lifts out** of the aircraft as a glowing amber block and transforms into a field of **3,000 EV battery packs** laid out on an airport apron in a grid that runs to the horizon.
- **Camera:** Top-down crane rising higher and higher to fit the grid.
- **MG:** `FULL TANKS ≈ 19 t OF FUEL` → `> 200 MWh OF ENERGY` → `≈ 3,000 EV BATTERIES`. A counter fills the grid.
- **Sound:** Liquid fill, then cascading electrical *clicks* (like dominoes) as the grid populates; bass hit on "three thousand".

### S3.8 · 2:10–2:15 · "…777 carries more than seven times that."
- **Visual:** A 777-class wide-body taxis across the grid. The grid **multiplies ×7** outward to the edge of the frame and beyond.
- **Camera:** Even higher crane; the airport becomes a tiny pattern.
- **MG:** `LONG-HAUL 777: × 7`.
- **Sound:** Riser, then a deep *BOOM*; music cuts to silence for the Section 4 transition.
- **Transition:** Light-flare wipe to black → the Energy Lab.

## SECTION 4 — JET FUEL VS BATTERIES (2:15–4:15) ★★★ THE CENTREPIECE
*Goal: the most visually impressive and clearest 2 minutes of the film. One set, one idea, escalating scale.*
*Set: **The Energy Lab**: a black infinite studio with a glossy black-stone floor, a single soft overhead key, thin amber and cyan practical rim lights. A floor-to-ceiling **light ruler** (etched kWh markings) stands at the back. Everything is physical and photoreal; graphics are rendered as light inside the set.*

### S4.1 · 2:15–2:19 · "So let's run an experiment."
- **Visual:** Lights come up in the Lab one at a time. A **precision balance scale** (brushed stainless beam, two glass platters, an analogue needle in a brass gauge) stands centre stage.
- **Camera:** Slow push-in from wide.
- **MG:** Chapter marker: `03 — FUEL vs BATTERY`.
- **Sound:** Each light = heavy theatrical *clunk* with long reverb. Room tone: deep, quiet.

### S4.2 · 2:19–2:27 · 1 kg fuel and 1 kg battery
- **Visual:** Left platter: a **graduated lab cylinder** with **1.25 L of clear, faintly straw-coloured jet fuel** (subtle meniscus, refraction, caustics on the platter). Right platter: a neat block of **14 cylindrical 21700 cells** (each ≈70 g), bundled with a nickel strip, labels visible: `21700 · Li-ion · 3.6 V`.
- **Camera:** Two matched macro inserts (100 mm, f/4): fuel surface ripple as it's set down; cells' metal sheen.
- **MG:** Labels: `JET FUEL · 1 kg` (amber) and `LITHIUM-ION CELLS · 1 kg` (cyan).
- **Sound:** Glass *clink* (fuel), solid metallic *clack* (cells). Electric hum fades in under the cells.

### S4.3 · 2:27–2:33 · "Same weight. Perfectly balanced."
- **Visual:** The beam wobbles, then **settles perfectly level**. The needle stops dead on zero.
- **Camera:** Locked wide frontal, perfectly symmetrical composition.
- **MG:** Needle readout: `1.000 kg | 1.000 kg`.
- **Sound:** Fine mechanical ticking settling to silence. One held breath of silence.

### S4.4 · 2:33–2:38 · Fuel: 12 kWh
- **Visual:** From the fuel cylinder, an **amber Energy Column** of light erupts upward, rising fast and **climbing off the top of the frame**.
- **Camera:** Tilts up with it (a fast but smooth tilt), the light ruler blurring past: 1… 2… 5… 10… stopping at **12 kWh**, far above the scale.
- **MG:** Ruler tick marks light amber as it passes. Final: `≈12 kWh`.
- **Sound:** Rising amber "burn" synth tone + whoosh, landing on a warm bass note.

### S4.5 · 2:38–2:43 · Battery: a quarter of one
- **Visual:** The camera descends all the way back down. From the cells, a **cyan Energy Column** rises… and stops **at knee height**.
- **Camera:** Tilts down; hold on the tiny cyan column next to the base of the towering amber column (the amber column exits frame top).
- **MG:** `≈0.25 kWh`.
- **Sound:** A short, small electric *zip* that stops abruptly. Silence.

### S4.6 · 2:43–2:47 · "around fifty times less"
- **Visual:** Wide shot: both columns side by side. The cyan column is a stub; the amber column is a skyscraper.
- **Camera:** Fast pull-back to fit the entire amber column in frame; the cyan column becomes a sliver.
- **MG:** Large type, centred: **`≈ 50×`**. Supporting line: `ENERGY PER KILOGRAM`.
- **Sound:** **Bass hit + sub drop**, the first big reveal.

### S4.7 · 2:47–2:57 · Engine efficiency (fair comparison)
- **Visual:** The amber column **splits**: about two-thirds of it peels off as **shimmering heat haze** (orange, rippling, dissipating upward like exhaust), labelled as loss. What remains is a shorter, dense amber core.
- **Camera:** Slow orbit around the amber column.
- **MG:** Small cutaway inset of the engine nozzle, showing hot exhaust. Labels: `LOST AS HEAT ≈ 2/3` · `USEFUL ≈ 1/3`. Remaining column reads `≈4 kWh USEFUL`.
- **Sound:** Heat-shimmer whoosh, low roar fading out.

### S4.8 · 2:57–3:02 · Electric motor 90%
- **Visual:** A beautifully rendered **electric motor cross-section** (copper windings, permanent magnets, rotor) spins up beside the cyan column. The cyan column loses only a thin sliver.
- **Camera:** Macro on copper windings, then back to the column.
- **MG:** `≈90% BECOMES MOTION`.
- **Sound:** Clean rising electric whine, very smooth.

### S4.9 · 3:02–3:09 · Cooling, wiring, casing
- **Visual:** The 14 cells **assemble into a module** in exploded view: cooling plate slides under, busbars clip on, a casing closes, a sensor harness snaps in. The battery's weight grows, so its column (now measured *per kilogram of complete pack*) shrinks a little more.
- **Camera:** Exploded-view assembly orbit (product-film style).
- **MG:** Parts labels pop in sync with VO: `COOLING` · `WIRING` · `CASING` · `PROTECTION`. Then `+ WEIGHT`.
- **Sound:** Precise mechanical clicks and snaps for each part (premium, Apple-like foley).

### S4.10 · 3:09–3:17 · "roughly twenty times"
- **Visual:** Both columns re-measured side by side as **useful energy per kg**: amber ≈4 kWh, cyan ≈0.2 kWh. Then the cyan column is **duplicated** to show it takes about **20 cyan columns stacked** to match one amber column; they stack, tick… tick… tick…, up to the amber top.
- **Camera:** Locked medium-wide; light ruler visible.
- **MG:** Counter ticking `1… 5… 10… 15… 20` as columns stack. Final: **`≈ 20×`** · `MORE USEFUL ENERGY PER KG`.
- **Sound:** Each stack = soft electric *tick* rising in pitch; final = **deep bass hit**.

### S4.11 · 3:17–3:23 · "Twenty times. That one number is the whole story."
- **Visual:** Everything in the Lab goes dark except the giant `20×` floating in the air, reflected in the black floor.
- **Camera:** Very slow push.
- **MG:** `20×` holds, then slides to the top-left corner, where it **stays pinned as a persistent bug** for the rest of Section 4 and all of Section 5.
- **Sound:** Silence → single low piano/synth note. Let it ring.

### S4.12 · 3:23–3:30 · One tonne of fuel (★ the hero comparison)
- **Visual:** Cut to a vast, clean **hangar**. In the centre, a **glass cube of jet fuel**: 1.25 m³, ≈1.08 m per side, glowing faintly amber from within, reflected in the polished floor. A person (technician in coveralls) stands beside it for scale: it reaches about mid-chest.
- **Camera:** Slow orbit around the cube, 35 mm.
- **MG:** Dimension lines snap on: `1 TONNE` · `≈1,250 LITRES` · `≈1.08 m`.
- **Sound:** Hangar ambience (big reverb, distant fan hum), liquid slosh as the cube settles.

### S4.13 · 3:30–3:37 · "…about twenty tonnes of batteries."
- **Visual:** Forklifts and a ceiling crane bring in **one-tonne battery pallet units** (realistic: grey steel enclosures, cyan status LEDs, orange HV cables, hazard labels). They land one after another beside the cube: **2… 5… 10… 20**, forming a **wall** that towers over the cube and the technician.
- **Camera:** Time-lapse feel: locked wide, with each pallet landing on beat; the final frame cranes up to show the wall dwarfing the cube.
- **MG:** Counter: `BATTERIES: 1 t → 20 t`. The **Load Gauge** appears for the first time (right side of frame), its needle climbing with each pallet.
- **Sound:** Each pallet: **heavy metallic impact** with floor rumble, layered with the electric hum getting thicker and louder. Hum swells into a riser.

### S4.14 · 3:37–3:43 · "…thirteen cars… one cube of fuel."
- **Visual:** Behind the battery wall, **13 ghosted car silhouettes** (realistic wireframe sedans) line up on a weighbridge, matching the wall's weight. Then cut back to the tiny fuel cube in the foreground, the battery wall out-of-focus behind.
- **Camera:** Rack focus from the wall to the cube, on the four stressed words.
- **MG:** `20 t ≈ 13 CARS`. Then simply: `1 CUBE OF FUEL` (amber) vs `20 t OF BATTERIES` (cyan).
- **Sound:** Four soft **bass pulses**, one per stressed word ("one / cube / of / fuel").

### S4.15 · 3:43–3:46 · "And there's a second problem…"
- **Visual:** Transition: the hangar doors open onto bright sky; whiteout.
- **Sound:** Whoosh + air rush.

### S4.16 · 3:46–3:56 · Fuel burns away (★ split-screen begins)
- **Visual:** **Split screen**, a vertical seam down the middle. Left: a 777-class jet in X-ray, **amber wing tanks visibly draining** (fluid sim, levels dropping). Right: an identical jet in X-ray, its belly and holds **packed with cyan battery modules**. Both fly in perfect formation over the ocean.
- **Camera:** Matched air-to-air side profiles, identical framing.
- **Note:** Illustrative. A battery 777 could never get airborne (see S5.9); here both start at the same weight so the *trend* is the only difference.
- **MG:** Each side has its own **Load Gauge** (digital readout + tape). A **flight-timer scrubber** runs along the bottom: `0 h → 13 h`. Left readout falls: `351 t → 251 t`. A line graph at bottom-left draws a **descending amber line** (weight vs time).
- **On-screen:** `≈100 TONNES LIGHTER AT LANDING`.
- **Sound:** Formation-flight drone in stereo (left side amber engine tone, right side cyan hum). A falling "fuel drain" glide tone on the left.

### S4.17 · 3:56–4:02 · "The flight gets easier…"
- **Visual:** Left jet: engine throttle visibly eases (less exhaust shimmer), the aircraft rises slightly (step-climb). A tiny arrow: `LESS THRUST NEEDED`.
- **Camera:** Gentle push on the left jet.
- **MG:** Fuel-burn-rate readout on the left decreases over time.
- **Sound:** Left engine note relaxes downward in pitch.

### S4.18 · 4:02–4:09 · "An empty battery weighs the same as a full one."
- **Visual:** Right jet: the cyan battery modules **dim** from bright cyan to dark grey as charge drains (100% → 0%), **but the weight readout does not move**. Insert: a single battery module on a scale, its charge display falling 100 → 0%, scale reading frozen.
- **Camera:** Push into the frozen right-side readout; then the module insert.
- **MG:** Right readout `LOCKED` with a small padlock glyph. Graph adds a **flat cyan line** next to the descending amber line, so the gap between them visibly widens.
- **On-screen:** `CHARGE: 100% → 0%` · `WEIGHT: UNCHANGED`.
- **Sound:** Electric hum *fading down* (pitch dropping) while a steady low drone holds: the sonic version of "weight stays". On "full one", a dry bass hit.

### S4.19 · 4:09–4:15 · "…from the runway all the way to the gate."
- **Visual:** Both jets land, side by side on parallel runways. Left: a light, gentle touchdown. Right: **heavy touchdown**, tyres smoke hard, oleo struts compress deep, a brief shudder through the airframe.
- **Camera:** Matched low runway angles, slight slow-motion (48→24 fps) on touchdown.
- **MG:** Final split readouts: `LANDS: 251 t` (amber) vs `LANDS: 351 t` (cyan → red tint).
- **Sound:** Light screech left; heavy thud + screech + metal stress groan right. Music hits a tense chord.
- **Transition:** Seam closes from both sides to a single frame: cut to Section 5.

## SECTION 5 — WHY THIS BREAKS DOWN FOR BIG JETS (4:15–5:30)
*Goal: escalation. Make the impossibility tangible, then absurd.*
*Darker grade, lower music; the `20×` bug stays pinned top-left.*

### S5.1 · 4:15–4:18 · "So let's try to build one."
- **Visual:** The hero A320 in a dark hangar under work lights, access panels open, a cargo door raised. Engineers' silhouettes. Mood: an engineering challenge.
- **Camera:** Slow lateral dolly.
- **MG:** Chapter marker: `04 — BUILDING AN ELECTRIC AIRLINER`.
- **Sound:** Hangar ambience, a distant impact wrench, a door rolling.

### S5.2 · 4:18–4:31 · Mission fuel: 4 t + reserves = 6 t
- **Visual:** Map Table: a 1,000 km route arc draws between two airports (generic cities). The route is then **extended** with a dashed diversion arc to an alternate airport and a **holding pattern** racetrack.
- **Camera:** Top-down, slow rotation.
- **MG:** Stacked fuel bar (amber): `TRIP ≈4 t` + `RESERVES ≈2 t` = **`≈6 t`**.
- **On-screen:** `RESERVES: DIVERT + HOLD (REQUIRED BY REGULATION)`.
- **Sound:** Map UI clicks; a soft radar-sweep tone during the holding pattern.

### S5.3 · 4:31–4:35 · "Six tonnes, times twenty…"
- **Visual:** The 6 t amber bar lifts off the map. The `20×` bug **flies down from the corner** and multiplies it.
- **MG:** `6 t × 20 =`
- **Sound:** Rising riser; tension.

### S5.4 · 4:35–4:38 · "…a hundred and twenty tonnes"
- **Visual:** **Hard cut** to the hangar: battery pallet units **rain down** around the A320, crane after crane, filling the floor.
- **MG:** Huge: **`120 t OF BATTERIES`**.
- **Sound:** **Biggest bass hit so far**, then pallet impacts.

### S5.5 · 4:38–4:47 · Over MTOW (★ load-gauge climax)
- **Visual:** X-ray mode: batteries **fill the cargo holds**, then the **cabin** (seats vanish row by row, replaced by modules), then the **wing box**. The **landing-gear oleos compress fully, tyres bulge**, the wing tips droop. The Load Gauge needle sweeps up past the **red MTOW line at 79 t** and keeps going.
- **Camera:** Slow orbit, low angle (makes the plane feel heavy), then a macro on a straining main-gear strut.
- **MG:** Load Gauge: `EMPTY AIRCRAFT 44 t` + `BATTERIES 120 t` = **`164 t`** vs red line `MAX TAKEOFF 79 t`. Gauge housing flashes red; cockpit-style caution: `OVERWEIGHT`. Bars show `PASSENGERS 0` · `BAGS 0`.
- **On-screen:** `MORE THAN TWICE THE PLANE'S MAXIMUM TAKEOFF WEIGHT`.
- **Sound:** Hydraulic strain, deep structural **creaks and groans**, a cockpit master-caution chime (two-tone, realistic, not cartoon), sub rumble.

### S5.6 · 4:47–5:02 · The weight spiral
- **Visual:** The aircraft lifts into a **dark abstract space** and becomes a translucent hologram. A circular **spiral diagram** forms around it in 3D: `MORE WEIGHT → BIGGER WINGS/STRUCTURE → MORE DRAG → MORE ENERGY → MORE BATTERIES → MORE WEIGHT…`. With each loop, the hologram's wings stretch wider and the battery mass grows, until the spiral **spins faster and the loop never closes** (the arrow overshoots the start point every lap).
- **Camera:** Slow top-down to 3/4 orbit, tracking the spiral.
- **MG:** Spiral text nodes light up in sequence (amber → red). Final label: **`THE WEIGHT SPIRAL`**, with `IT NEVER CLOSES` underneath.
- **Sound:** A looping rhythmic pulse that **accelerates** with each lap; Shepard-tone riser (sounds endlessly rising), cut off abruptly.
